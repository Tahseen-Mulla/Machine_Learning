What is K-Means Clustering?

K-Means is an unsupervised algorithm that partitions unlabelled data into k groups, minimising the within-cluster variance:

Repeat until convergence:
1. Assign — put each point in the cluster of its nearest centroid.
2. Update — move each centroid to the mean position of its assigned points.

The algorithm minimises inertia (the sum of squared distances from points to their cluster's centroid). It requires k to be chosen in advance — the elbow method (plotting inertia against several values of k and looking for where the improvement flattens) is a common way to pick a good k. K-Means is also sensitive to the initial centroid positions, which is why it's often run several times from different random starts.
